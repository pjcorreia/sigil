01 — The Dentsu Empire in Portugal

Dentsu Group (Tokyo-listed, ~$10B revenue) entered the Portuguese market through the 2018 acquisition of Partners, the country's largest independent creative agency founded by Tomás Froes in 2003. The acquisition folded Partners into the Dentsu network as Dentsu Creative Portugal.

In 2024, operations were restructured into Dentsu Creative Iberia, merging Portugal and Spain under Froes as CEO and Lourenço Thomaz as Chief Creative Officer. Patrick Stilwell serves as General Director for Portugal.

The acquisition guaranteed continuity.

Partners had already won multiple consecutive Turismo de Portugal creative tenders before Dentsu acquired them. The acquisition didn't disrupt the incumbency — it reinforced it with global resources. As the founders themselves stated: Dentsu would "open doors to the world."

03 — The Incumbency Machine

Tourism promotion contracts create natural incumbency advantages: deep knowledge of the destination brand, established media relationships, and the political cost of changing creative direction mid-campaign. Partners/Dentsu exploited this fully.

The "Can't Skip" franchise.

The "Can't Skip Portugal" / "Can't Skip Hope" campaign (launched March 2020 by Partners) won Gold at Marketing Awards for Travel & Tourism. Campaign recognition creates brand equity — and makes it politically difficult for Turismo de Portugal to switch agencies without appearing to abandon a successful creative platform.

The 80% Problem

Portugal awards approximately 80% of public contracts without competition (direct awards). For cultural heritage tenders specifically, the rate reaches 92%. Of the remaining competitive tenders, 61% receive only one bid. This is not a Dentsu-specific problem — it's the system within which Dentsu operates. But it means that even when tenders are held, the competitive pressure is minimal.

04 — Tourism Dependency

Portugal's tourism exports reached €27.7 billion in 2024, representing 9.7% of GDP. This makes Portugal one of the most tourism-dependent economies in the EU (after Croatia, Malta, and Cyprus). The promotion of Portuguese tourism is not a minor line item — it's a strategic national priority.

High stakes, low transparency.

When an industry represents nearly 10% of GDP, the contracts that promote it should face the highest level of scrutiny. Instead, Portugal's tourism promotion spending flows through a procurement system where the vast majority of contracts bypass competitive bidding entirely.

05 — What Nobody Is Talking About

1. Total lifetime spend unknown.

The combined value of all Partners/Dentsu contracts with Turismo de Portugal across 10+ years has never been publicly calculated. The confirmed €20M+ likely represents only a fraction of the total relationship.

2. No campaign ROI metrics published.

Despite spending tens of millions on tourism promotion, Turismo de Portugal has never published cost-per-visitor-acquired or campaign effectiveness metrics. Without ROI data, it's impossible to assess whether the spending is justified — let alone whether a different agency might achieve more for less.

3. Losing bidders silenced.

Details of competing bids are not typically disclosed in Portuguese procurement. Agencies that lost tenders to Partners/Dentsu have not spoken publicly — either because they hope for future contracts or because the market is too small to risk antagonizing a major client.

4. The Iberia consolidation question.

The 2024 merger into Dentsu Creative Iberia raises a question: if the same leadership now manages both Portuguese and Spanish accounts, how does this affect competitive dynamics when Portugal and Spain are competing for the same tourists?
