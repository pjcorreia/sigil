Methodology

How SIGIL collects data, identifies corruption patterns, and classifies procurement fraud risk in Portuguese public contracts.

01 — Data Sources

SIGIL analysis relies on four complementary public data sources:

BASE.gov.pt — Portuguese Public Procurement Data Portal

The primary database. BASE.gov.pt publishes all public contracts above €5,000 (Law 19/2012), including:

  • Procurement procedure type (open, restricted, negotiated, direct)
  • Initial and final contract value
  • Publication, award, and completion dates
  • Awarding and awarded entities
  • Number of competitors

Inclusion in BASE is mandatory and data is structured — enabling automated mass analysis of 100,000s of contracts.

Tribunal de Contas — Court of Auditors Reports

The Tribunal de Contas (TdC) publishes periodic audit reports on public entities and public utilities. These reports contain:

  • Findings on financial mismanagement
  • Irregular spending patterns
  • Compliance recommendations
  • Penalties and sanctions imposed

TdC reports are authoritative documents — they often identify suspicious behavior years before criminal action.

DGRM — Directorate-General for Administrative Reform

DGRM maintains records of organizational structure of public entities, administrative reforms, restructurings, and legislative milestones. Critical for:

  • Tracking parent entities and subsidiaries
  • Identifying when structures are created/dissolved
  • Understanding governance architecture

TED (Tenders Electronic Daily) — EU Database

TED publishes EU public contracts above €140,000. For cross-border Portuguese contracts and EU co-funded projects, TED provides additional visibility and independent timestamps.

02 — Analysis Methodology

Data Extraction and Cleaning

BASE.gov.pt is crawled periodically. Data is:

  • Downloaded in structured JSON/CSV format
  • Normalized (entity names harmonized, currency values in EUR, dates in ISO format)
  • Duplicates identified and removed
  • Missing fields marked; incomplete records flagged

Identification of Suspicious Entities

Analysis begins by identifying entities with anomalous procurement patterns:

  • Lack of Competition: Contracts with 0–1 competitors (expected <5% in "open" procedures; >50% suggests biased selection)
  • Price Jumps: Contracts where final value is 200%+ of initial (indicator of expansion without renewed competition)
  • Low-Capital Entities: Awarded with registered capital <€50,000 but win €1M+ contracts (shell company flag)
  • Relationship Pattern: Companies that only win contracts from one specific entity (anomalous subordination)
  • Suspicious Timing: Companies created days before winning a contract, or dissolved days after

Pattern Detection

After identifying suspicious entities, analysis searches for structural patterns:

  • Subsidiary Networks: Search for entities that function jointly (same shareholders, same addresses, overlapping board members)
  • Legal Fronts: Companies registered in opaque jurisdictions, with management distant from "home country"
  • Money Flow Cycles: Tracking how money flows from public entity → awarded company → other entities (indicator of laundering or evasion)
  • Governance Overlap: Board members, shareholders, or administrators appearing simultaneously across multiple entities

03 — Classification System

Each investigation is classified into one of three states:

UNREPORTED

Corruption patterns identified through public data, but never investigated by authorities. Suspicious behavior is evident (lack of competition, massive contract expansion, shell entities, governance links), but remains publicly uninvestigated — suggests gaps in oversight.

REPORTED

Authorities have initiated formal investigation. May be ongoing (PJ, DCIAP, TdC). Investigation is confirmed via press releases, official reports, or public legal action.

ENFORCED

Conviction, penalty, or sanction imposed. Rests on final court judgment, administrative act, or audit report with executed fine. Evidence of accountability.

Note on Presumption of Innocence

No classification implies guilt. UNREPORTED means "not publicly investigated," not "guilty." Listed entities have the right to contest data and correct records — see "How to Contribute Corrections" section below.

04 — Limitations and Caveats

AI-Generated Analysis

Investigation pages are generated by large language models (LLMs). While based on reliable public data, summaries, analyses, and conclusions may contain:

  • Factual Inaccuracies: LLMs occasionally distort dates, numbers, or event sequences
  • Exaggerated Inferences: Models may suggest connections that are speculative rather than direct
  • Lost Context: Explanations may be oversimplified

All numbers, dates, and claims should be independently verified using the sources provided.

Potential Errors

  • BASE.gov.pt Defects: If the public data platform contains errors or incomplete records, analysis is affected
  • Name Confusion: May be confusion between companies with similar names, especially if addresses differ slightly
  • Incomplete Governance Context: Complex ownership structures (trusts, foundations, intermediaries) may be hidden from Portuguese public records
  • Data Timing: BASE.gov.pt has publication delays; very recent contracts may not yet be registered

Need for Human Verification

This platform is an initial investigation tool, not a conclusion. Journalists, researchers, and regulators should:

  • Validate all numbers against original sources
  • Seek corroborating documentation (emails, memos, official decisions)
  • Give entities opportunity to respond before publication
  • Be aware that correlation does not imply causation

Not Covered

This analysis focuses on quantitative data patterns. Does not cover:

  • Technical quality of work delivered (a contract may lack competition but the company may be doing good work)
  • Legitimate reasons for negotiated procedures (e.g., unique expertise, system compatibility)
  • Private transactions between companies (only public data are visible)
  • Motivation or intent — only external behavior is analyzed
