01 — What is SUCH?

Serviço de Utilização Comum dos Hospitais (SUCH) was founded on April 22, 1966 under Decree-Law No. 46,668/1965 as a non-profit private association with public utility status. Its mission: provide shared services — nutrition, engineering, cleaning, maintenance, logistics, waste management — to Portugal's public hospital network.

With approximately 3,250 employees and headquartered at Parque de Saúde de Lisboa, SUCH serves dozens of public health entities including hospitals, health centers, and regional health administrations. Its founding pioneer was Professor Coriolano Albino Ferreira (1916–1996).

On paper, a shared-services model should reduce costs. In practice, SUCH became a vehicle for concealing debt, awarding contracts without competition, and channeling public health money through opaque shell subsidiaries with as little as €0 in registered capital.

02 — The Shell Subsidiary Network

SUCH operated through a network of Agrupamentos Complementares de Empresas (ACE) — joint venture-like structures that allowed it to compartmentalize activities and, critically, hide debt off its own balance sheet.

Somos Pessoas was registered with a capital of just nine euros — less than the cost of a hospital meal — yet it coordinated personnel services for Lisbon's hospital network. This is not a startup. It's a vehicle for separating liabilities from assets.

The €0 company handling procurement.

Somos Compras — designated an official public purchasing center by Decreto-Lei 200/2008 — had a registered capital of zero. Despite this, it accumulated €19.3M in banking debt. When it imploded, Lisbon hospital centers paid 100% of this debt despite holding only 9% participation. The remaining 91% holder — SUCH — walked away, receiving an additional €3.7M in debt forgiveness.

A fourth entity, Somos Ambiente, was later implicated in the EU fraud investigation (Operation Inovar). This subsidiary structure served a dual purpose: operational compartmentalization, and shielding SUCH's own books from the true scale of its financial exposure — €17.6M hidden across the Somos entities.

03 — The €45.5 Million Cover-Up

The Tribunal de Contas (Court of Auditors) Report No. 30/2015 revealed that SUCH had concealed a liability of €45.5 million in its 2013–2014 financial accounts. The organization had reached a state of technical bankruptcy with negative equity of €36.3M — a fact hidden from the hospitals it served and the public it was meant to protect.

Fine for concealing €45.5M: just €510.

The Tribunal de Contas identified massive accounting irregularities requiring financial restructuring. The penalty? A fine of five hundred and ten euros. For context, this is equivalent to fining someone 0.0011% of the amount they concealed — or roughly the cost of parking at a Lisbon hospital for a month.

How the Debt Was Hidden

The ACE subsidiaries served as off-balance-sheet vehicles. Somos Compras alone carried €19.3M in banking debt, but because it was a separate legal entity with its own books, this debt didn't appear on SUCH's consolidated accounts. When the structure collapsed, the hospitals — funded by SNS (National Health Service) budget — absorbed the losses while SUCH's leadership faced no meaningful consequences.

Who Paid?

The Lisbon hospital centers, holding just 9% of Somos Compras, paid 100% of the €19.3M banking debt through SNS budget allocations via a 10-year recourse agreement. SUCH received €3.7M in debt forgiveness. In 2015, the government orchestrated a €17.2M transfer of legal positions from the Somos entities to SPMS (Serviços Partilhados do Ministério da Saúde) — effectively socializing the losses.

05 — Operation Inovar: EU Fraud Charges

In May 2019, Portugal's Polícia Judiciária launched Operation Inovar, a national operation targeting fraudulent use of EU "Portugal 2020" structural funds. SUCH's president Paulo Jorge Rendeiro Correia de Sousa was among 73+ individuals constituted as suspects.

Charged with EU fraud — then reappointed president.

Paulo Sousa was constituted as a defendant on May 2–3, 2019 in connection with fraudulent invoicing schemes to obtain EU subsidies through Somos Ambiente. The charges include document falsification, money laundering, and tax fraud. Despite this, he was reappointed President of SUCH on May 2, 2022 via Despacho 5212/2022 — exactly three years after his indictment.

07 — What Nobody Is Talking About

The Tribunal de Contas audits and Operation Inovar have received some media coverage. But several critical angles remain completely unreported:

1. The €9 / €0 shell structure.

No Portuguese media has investigated why public hospital services were channeled through entities with zero or single-digit registered capital. This is a textbook shell company pattern — minimal capitalization to limit liability while handling millions in public funds.

2. The reappointment paradox.

Paulo Sousa was charged with EU fraud in 2019 and reappointed president in 2022. No media outlet has questioned how someone under active criminal investigation for defrauding EU funds was deemed suitable to lead a public-utility organization handling NHS money.

3. The €510 fine for €45.5M.

The fine represents 0.0011% of the concealed amount. This ratio — and what it says about accountability in Portuguese public governance — has not been examined as a systemic issue.

4. Contract expansion patterns.

SUCH contracts that expanded by up to 11,000% of original value through modifications remain unexamined. The mechanism — win a small contract, then expand it without new competition — is a procurement red flag that BASE.gov.pt data should reveal.
